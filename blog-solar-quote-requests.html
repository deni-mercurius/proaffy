Why Solar Quote Requests Go Cold

July 1, 2026 • 7 min read

A homeowner who asks for a solar quote has almost always asked three other companies in the same sitting. Everything after that is a race nobody told you had started.

The Race You Were Not Told About

Four installers got that enquiry. Possibly five. The homeowner filled in one form, then the next, then the next, in a single evening on a phone, and by the time the first of you replies they have already started forming an opinion about who is organised and who is not.

Nobody tells you this. The enquiry arrives in your inbox looking like a private conversation.

Where the Conversation Actually Dies

Not at the price. That is the comfortable explanation and it is usually wrong. It dies in the two weeks between the first reply and the site survey, when the initial enthusiasm has worn off, the homeowner is reading forum threads about inverter brands, and nobody has contacted them since Tuesday.

Harvard Business Review audited 2,241 US companies to see how long each took to answer a web enquiry. Twenty-three percent never answered at all. The average reply, among those that did answer within a month, took 42 hours. In a four-way comparison that is not a slow start. It is a forfeit.

A separate study at MIT Sloan found that contacting a lead five minutes after it arrives rather than thirty makes qualifying it twenty-one times more likely. Five minutes against thirty. Not five minutes against two days.

Speed and Persistence Are Different Problems

Most advice about lead response stops at speed, which suits businesses where the decision happens in one call. Solar is not one of those. You need both halves: a reply fast enough to be first, and a follow-up patient enough to still be there when the homeowner finally talks to their partner about it.

The second half is where almost everyone stops. It is easy to automate a fast first message and much harder to keep a thread alive across six weeks without becoming the company that pesters people.

What a Qualified Solar Lead Actually Needs

Before anyone drives to a site survey, a handful of things decide whether the afternoon is worth spending:

  • Whether they own the roof, which rules out a surprising share of enquiries in one question.
  • Roof age and orientation, because a replacement in two years changes the entire conversation.
  • Obvious shading, which the homeowner can usually describe better than a satellite image can.
  • Roughly what they pay now, since payback is the number they are actually shopping.

None of that requires an engineer. It requires somebody asking, quickly, while the homeowner is still thinking about it.

The Part You Can Fix This Month

You cannot make the decision faster. Nobody buys an array in an afternoon and it would be strange if they did. What you can change is whether you are in the conversation at all when it finally concludes, and that is decided in the first minute and then defended for six weeks.

Getting found by homeowners who are comparing installers is the other half of the work, and it is covered in local search and getting cited by AI assistants. Neither helps if the enquiry they produce sits unanswered until Thursday.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast should a solar company reply to a quote request?

Before the other installers do. The homeowner submitted several forms in one sitting, so the first useful reply gets to frame what matters: roof orientation, shading, and what the utility actually pays for an export. Everyone who replies after that is answering your questions.

If the sales cycle is weeks, why does the first minute matter?

Because the first minute decides whether there is a cycle. Being first does not win the job on its own. It buys you the position of the company the homeowner is comparing the others against, which is a different conversation entirely.

What about quotes that went quiet months ago?

They are usually the cheapest work available. Rates move, incentives change, and a proposal that got no answer in spring is not the same offer in autumn. Most installers never reopen them.
