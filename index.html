The problem

Most enquiries are answered too late to matter

Harvard Business Review audited 2,241 US companies to find out how long each took to answer a web enquiry. 37 percent replied inside an hour. 23 percent never replied at all. Among those that did answer within a month, the average reply took 42 hours.

A separate study at MIT Sloan found that contacting a lead five minutes after it arrives, rather than thirty, makes qualifying it twenty-one times more likely.

By then the homeowner has called somebody else. Nothing about this is a failure of effort: you were working, and the phone was in a van two streets away.

Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, The Short Life of Online Sales Leads, Harvard Business Review 89(3), March 2011. The response-time multipliers come from the MIT Sloan and InsideSales study by the same lead author, which is widely miscredited to Harvard.

The sequence

What happens in the first sixty seconds

  • 00:00 A homeowner submits the form on your site, or misses you on the phone.
  • 00:03 The enquiry reaches the queue and is matched to your service area.
  • 00:12 The first message goes out from your number, using their name and what they asked about.
  • 00:47 They reply, because a text arriving in under a minute still feels like a person.
  • 00:60 A qualifying question goes back, and the booking either starts or does not.

Three things can happen instead, and the sequence handles each rather than pretending they do not occur. No answer after three attempts, spread across two days. A clear no, which closes the thread. An address outside the radius you gave us, which never reaches your calendar.
